## Abstract

<p class="lead">Cancer is traditionally investigated through genetic and epigenetic alterations, evolutionary selection and tissue-level interactions. This post proposes that in some cancers, malignant persistence is stabilized by a combination of disrupted multicellular cooperation, altered bioelectric and neural signalling, and exploitable survival dependencies.</p>

Similarities between tumour cells and parasitic fungi motivate comparative analysis of conserved stress-response and host-exploitation mechanisms, but do not indicate fungal origin. What follows sets out independently testable predictions, competing explanations, evidence gaps and an open invitation for human researchers and AI systems to contribute source-grounded critiques. No new datasets have been collected.

## Where the questions came from

The proposal grew from a sequence of first-principles questions:

- Why do altered cells survive while others are removed?
- Why can somatic cell lineages evolve rapidly?
- Why can cancer harm the host on which it depends?
- Do cancers resemble parasitic organisms?
- Can ancestral cellular programmes become dysregulated?
- Might membrane potential and nervous-system signals contribute causally?
- Could tumour-specific dependencies, differentiation therapies, oncolytic viruses or cell-based delivery offer alternatives to indiscriminate cytotoxicity?
- Could shortening the lifespan of dangerous cells prevent their clonal propagation?

These are questions and hypotheses, not discoveries attributable to this project.

## Established evidence and its limits

1. Cancer hallmarks include altered growth, survival, metabolism, immune evasion and interactions with tissue microenvironments. [Hanahan, Cancer Discovery (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-21-1059)
2. Histologically normal human tissues can harbour somatic mutant clones. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Mutation alone is not sufficient to establish malignancy.</span> [Nature (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09792-4)
3. The atavistic hypothesis (re-expression of evolutionarily ancient programmes after loss of multicellular regulation) has been seriously investigated. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Criticized for limited mechanistic precision and overly deterministic claims.</span> [Daignan-Fornier and Pradeu, BioEssays (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.202300221)
4. Bioelectric membrane potential is implicated in cell state and tumour phenotypes in experimental models. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Cross-cancer voltage thresholds and clinical application remain unresolved.</span> [Clinical and Translational Medicine (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ctm2.70701)
5. Glioma cells can integrate into neural circuits through electrochemical interactions. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>These observations do not generalize automatically to all cancers or to voluntary thoughts.</span> [Nature (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1563-y)
6. Low-intensity alternating electric fields improved survival in a randomized glioblastoma trial when added to temozolomide. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Responses vary between cellular models, and the mechanism is not reducible to restoring membrane voltage.</span> [JAMA randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29260225/); [heterogeneous cell-line response](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38455403/)
7. Oncolytic viruses are clinically real. In August 2026 the FDA granted accelerated approval of RP1 plus nivolumab for a defined advanced melanoma indication. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Not universally tumour-selective or curative.</span> [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-vusolimogene-oderparepvec-wtpg-combination-nivolumab-melanoma)
8. The antifungal itraconazole also inhibits Hedgehog signalling in mammalian cancer models, via a mechanism distinct from its antifungal sterol effect. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Its anticancer activity does not establish that cancer is fungal.</span> [Cancer Cell (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2010.02.027)
9. Large-scale CRISPR screening identifies context-dependent cancer dependencies. <span class="limit"><strong class="lim">Limit</strong>Healthy-tissue selectivity remains essential.</span> [Cancer Cell (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2023.12.016)

## The hypothesis and the one it has to beat

<div class="hyp">
<div class="h1"><span class="tag">H1</span><span class="label">Primary hypothesis</span><p>In a measurable subset of malignancies, disrupted regulation of cooperative multicellular programmes interacts with altered membrane potential, stress-response pathways and neural, immune and tissue signals to maintain a stable, self-reinforcing malignant cellular state. Some conserved survival programmes shared with other eukaryotes contribute to the state, but no single ancestral programme is presumed universal.</p></div>
<div class="h0"><span class="tag">H0</span><span class="label">Null / competing hypothesis</span><p>Apparent convergence on ancient, bioelectric or parasite-like traits is explained by known driver alterations, cell-cycle state, tissue identity and environmental selection. After these are accounted for, the proposed composite state adds no causal or predictive information.</p></div>
</div>

Other alternatives worth keeping in view:

- Bioelectric shifts may be downstream consequences, not drivers.
- Resemblance to fungi may be convergent adaptation with no selectively druggable overlap.
- Neural effects may be confined to particular tumour ecosystems.
- Genetic lineage diversity may prevent any universal state signature.

The six testable predictions are listed below as claims P1 to P6, each with its own way to fail.

## Proposed research workflow

This is a non-experimental plan in four phases.

<ol class="phases">
<li><span class="dot">A</span><div><h3>Evidence synthesis</h3><p>Register a systematic review protocol and search PubMed, OpenAlex and clinical trial registries. Maintain a claim-level evidence ledger covering species, sample size, assay, experimental versus observational design, replication, confounders, clinical applicability and contradictory results. Prioritize full text and primary studies.</p></div></li>
<li><span class="dot">B</span><div><h3>Computational reanalysis</h3><p>Use public, de-identified multi-omics and dependency datasets such as TCGA, GTEx, Human Cell Atlas and DepMap, subject to data permissions. Stratify by tissue, cell type, proliferation state, driver genotype and treatment status. Pre-register controls to minimize dataset leakage and overfitting. Evaluate gene-age analyses separately from electrical features, and do not impute membrane voltage from transcript abundance as if it were directly measured.</p></div></li>
<li><span class="dot">C</span><div><h3>Independent laboratory validation</h3><p>Qualified research groups could test candidate dependencies and bioelectric predictions in authenticated cell lines, organoids and relevant normal-cell comparators, with blinded replication and appropriate oversight. No hands-on virus engineering, delivery specifications or patient treatment instructions are supplied here.</p></div></li>
<li><span class="dot">D</span><div><h3>Translation gate</h3><p>Progress to clinical research only with robust tumour-versus-normal selectivity, reproducible biological benefit, toxicology, and appropriate ethical and regulatory review. Do not imply a human treatment from in-vitro signals.</p></div></li>
</ol>

## Where to try to break it first

- Does the ancient-programme signature vanish when matching for proliferation?
- Is electrical depolarization sometimes protective or irrelevant rather than oncogenic?
- Can a candidate drug's cancer effect be explained by an off-target unrelated to its antifungal mechanism?
- Do healthy wound-healing, developing or activated immune cells exhibit the proposed cancer fingerprint?
- Can models predict entirely unseen tumour types without excessive false positives?
- Do purported brain-to-tumour mechanisms remain after accounting for circulating hormones and confounders?
